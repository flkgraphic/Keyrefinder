Lost Your Keys in Switzerland – What Now?

It's one of those moments nobody wants to experience: you reach into your pocket and your keys are gone. Losing your keys in Switzerland can be stressful — a lost key can mean high costs and the hassle of replacing locks.

But stay calm! In most cases, lost keys turn up again — especially when a smart key return system is in place. We've put together an immediate checklist of everything you need to do when your keys go missing.

Checklist: what to do when your keys are gone

Work through these steps systematically to maximise your chances of getting them back:

1. Stay calm and retrace your steps

Often the key is still at a place you visited recently. Mentally retrace your last movements. Were you at a restaurant, on the train or at work? Call those places and ask.

2. Is there a KeyRefinder on your key ring?

If your key ring has a KeyRefinder® or KeyShopper® attached, your chances are excellent! Many honest finders use the system. A finder can simply drop the key in a post box or contact us through the system. Wait 1–2 days — in many cases we'll be in touch soon with the good news. How it works exactly: How the KeyRefinder system works.

3. Contact lost property offices and the police

Don't have a KeyRefinder? Then contact the lost property offices in your municipality or city. If you lost your keys on an SBB train or at a station, check the SBB electronic lost property database (EasyFind). A query at the local police station can also be worthwhile. For finders, we have a separate guide: Found a key – what to do?

4. Inform your landlord or property management

If the key belongs to a rental property in Switzerland, you must notify your property management or landlord. This is especially important if you lost the key near your home or if an address tag was attached to the ring, due to the risk of break-in.

Frequently asked questions (FAQ) for key losers

Does insurance cover lost keys?

In Switzerland, private liability insurance often covers the cost of replacing the lock cylinder if you've lost a key for someone else's property (e.g. a rental flat). However, always check your policy carefully, as "losing" a key (as opposed to theft) can often be treated as own damage or gross negligence.

How long does it take to get a lost KeyRefinder key back?

It depends on how quickly the key is found. If a finder drops the key into a Swiss Post letterbox, it typically arrives at our secure centre within 2–3 working days. We'll contact you immediately.

Do I absolutely need to change my lock?

Replacing the lock is strongly recommended if the key was lost together with ID documents (name/address) or if you lost it directly in front of your home. If the key is completely anonymous (e.g. only fitted with a KeyRefinder) and was lost far from your home, the risk of break-in is extremely low. When in doubt, discuss this with your insurer or property manager.
